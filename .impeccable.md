## Design Context

### Users
Agencies, freelancers, QA teams, and accessibility consultants auditing many client sites. Their job is to find deterministic accessibility defects across real, often authenticated flows, communicate evidence to a client, and prevent regressions in CI without collecting client passwords.

### Brand Personality
Forensic, precise, and candid. The interface should create confidence through inspectable evidence and clear boundaries: axe-core produces compliance-relevant violations; the persona layer produces labeled task-success and usability opinion. It must feel like a diagnostic instrument, not a chatbot or an overlay vendor.

### Aesthetic Direction
Dark-first warm-neutral evidence terminal with a paper-lab light variant. Use JetBrains Mono for instrumentation and labels, restrained sans copy, and Source Serif report prose. The signature visual is a live audit log and replay evidence, with compact flat panels, small radii, borders instead of shadows, and teal reserved for active/live state. Avoid generic SaaS cards, gradients, glass, neon, fake social proof, and disability-simulation imagery.

### Design Principles
1. Show evidence and real product behavior before marketing claims.
2. Preserve the verdict, task-success, and opinion boundary in every interaction and label.
3. Give each page one clear primary job and make the next step explicit.
4. Design for dense professional work while retaining a calm, legible first-run experience.
5. Treat accessibility states, mobile adaptation, loading, errors, and reduced motion as core product design.
